THE SYDNEY ECLIPSE ARCHIVE • HISTORICAL RECORD

1857

The previous total solar eclipse over Sydney.

Before aircraft, live event centres, digital countdowns and SydneyEclipse, the city had already experienced totality. In 1857, the Moon's shadow reached Sydney around sunrise, bringing a total solar eclipse during the early morning.

For about two minutes around sunrise, Sydney experienced totality — a very different setting from the afternoon eclipse that will cross the city in 2028.

The event belongs to a very different era of eclipse observation. This part of the site therefore leaves the modern Otono red and purple behind: parchment, ink, serif type and an astronomical-record layout take over.

For Sydney, it became the beginning of an extraordinary wait. The next total solar eclipse over the city would not arrive until 2028.

THE CLOUDS WON.

Heavy cloud covered the Sun and Moon during totality, hiding the corona from view. Rather than presenting 1857 as a perfect predecessor to 2028, the archive remembers it as a total eclipse whose most dramatic sight was concealed by the weather.
