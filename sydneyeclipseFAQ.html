3. What actually happens during totality?+
The Moon completely covers the Sun's bright disc. Daylight rapidly fades, the sky darkens, and the normally hidden solar corona becomes visible around the Moon. This is fundamentally different from even an extremely deep partial eclipse.
4. When was Sydney's last total solar eclipse?+
1857.
Sydney's previous total solar eclipse occurred in 1857. But there was a major problem.
☁️ HEAVY CLOUD.
The clouds heavily covered the Moon and Sun, making the corona completely invisible during roughly 2 minutes of totality.

5. Does the cloudy 1857 eclipse still count as a total eclipse?+
Yes. Weather affects whether observers can see an eclipse, not whether the astronomical event occurs. The Moon's shadow still crossed Sydney even though cloud ruined the view from the ground.

7. Does all of Australia experience totality in 2028?+
No. Totality occurs only inside the Moon's umbral path. Places outside it see a partial eclipse.

SEE THE AUSTRALIAN PATH →
8. Where does the 2028 path cross Australia?+
The path runs from the Kimberley region, through the Northern Territory, south-west Queensland and New South Wales, before reaching Greater Sydney.
KIMBERLEY → NT → QUEENSLAND → NSW → SYDNEY 🌑
9. Where in Australia gets the longest totality?+
In this site's location dataset, the Kimberley Region reaches 5m 10s.

📍 SYDNEY — ~3m 50s
10. Is totality the same length everywhere?+
No. Duration changes by location within the shadow. In the supplied locations it ranges from 5m 10s in Kimberley to 1m 36s at Kiama.

11. Is Sydney exactly on the centreline?+
Different parts of Greater Sydney receive slightly different durations: Penrith 3m 46s, Blacktown 3m 49s, Sydney 3m 49s, Darlinghurst 3m 49s, Mosman 3m 48s, and Gosford 3m 19s.
12. Why do different Sydney suburbs have different eclipse times?+
Because the Moon's shadow moves across Earth. Totality reaches different parts of Greater Sydney at slightly different times.
13. How long does the entire eclipse last?+
Approximately 12:40 → 15:14. But totality occupies only a tiny fraction of that time.

14. Is the Sun completely hidden for the entire eclipse?+
NO. 😭
The overall eclipse lasts for more than two hours, but the Sun is completely covered only during totality. Before and after it, some of the bright solar disc remains visible.
15. Do I need eclipse glasses?+
Proper solar-viewing protection is required whenever any part of the bright solar disc remains visible. Use appropriate eclipse-viewing equipment and recognised solar-viewing safety guidance.

16. Can clouds ruin a total solar eclipse?+
Unfortunately, yes. Thick cloud can hide the Sun, Moon and corona from observers.
